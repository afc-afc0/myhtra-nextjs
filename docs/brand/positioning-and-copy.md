# Myhtra Studios: positioning and homepage copy

Research-backed positioning, principles and ready-to-paste copy for myhtra.co.
Audience: CTOs, founders and engineering leads deciding whether to hand us a project.
Every number below comes from Ahmet's own record. Nothing is invented: no clients, testimonials, dates or capacity.

Items marked **[confirm]** need Ahmet's sign-off before launch (see section 5).

---

## 1. Positioning

**Statement**

> For CTOs and founders who need AI agents, cloud platforms and web apps that hold up in production,
> Myhtra Studios is a founder-led engineering studio in Toronto that designs, builds and runs them end to end,
> with security and human approval built in rather than bolted on. You work directly with a senior engineer
> who has shipped enterprise systems since 2019. No hand-offs, no account managers.

The thread that makes this ours and not generic: **automation you can trust.** It runs through the facts:
the PwC provisioning agent gated by human approval, DailyFlow's agent that asks before it acts, the Book
studio where every line is approved, the 20+ vulnerabilities fixed to pass an audit, and DreamAuth's data residency.

**Headline options**

| # | Headline | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **AI agents and cloud platforms you can put in production.** | Says what we build and the outcome buyers worry about (will it survive production?) | Web apps move to the subhead |
| B | Software, cloud and AI agents that just work. | Short, warm | "Just work" is a claim every studio makes, so it fails the specificity test |
| C | Senior engineering for teams shipping AI, cloud and web, end to end. | Clear about who it's for | Reads a little like a staffing agency |

**Pick: A.** It names the offer and the outcome in under 10 words, and "production" is the word our buyers worry about.

## 2. Principles

1. **State the value proposition within 10 seconds.** Users usually leave within 10–20 seconds, and
   NN/g's guidance is "you must clearly communicate your value proposition within 10 seconds."
   → The hero answers *what we build, for whom, why us* in one headline plus one sentence. No carousel or
   rotating words, and the terminal animation never replaces the headline.
   Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/

2. **Be specific and objective, not promotional.** In NN/g's study, objective copy scored 27% better on
   usability than "marketese", and concise + scannable + objective together scored 124% better. Users said they
   "detest anything that seems like marketing fluff."
   → Swap adjectives for nouns and numbers: "20 legacy .NET apps moved to .NET 6/8", not "revolutionary
   modernization". Drop "perfection is our goal" from body copy and keep the motto as a sign-off only.
   Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/

3. **One primary CTA, labelled with what happens next.** Vague labels like "Get Started" "can apply to almost
   any goal" and pull clicks from the wrong people. Too many options raise the odds of *choice deferral*
   (people decide later, which means never), especially when the decision is complex.
   → One primary action everywhere: **Start a project**. One quiet secondary: **See the work**. Under the
   button, one line saying what it does ("Opens an email to Ahmet").
   Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/get-started/ ·
   Iyengar & Lepper 2000, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.995 ·
   Chernev et al. 2015 meta-analysis (effect depends on complexity and uncertainty):
   https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2015/when-product-assortment-leads-to-choice-overload-a-conceptual

4. **Build authority from evidence, not self-description.** Cialdini: you can't easily announce your own
   brilliance, so let credentials and third parties speak. Visible markers of expertise raise compliance.
   → Proof bar of hard numbers, named environments (with permission), and *this site itself* as a live
   demo (Next.js 16 on AKS, Helm, Keycloak). No "expert" or "world-class" adjectives.
   Source: https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/

5. **Honest social proof works without testimonials.** Social proof works best as honest information about
   what similar people did (Cialdini's towel study: +33%). B2B buyers want proof of business benefit, such as case
   studies in a Challenge → Solution → Result shape across varied industries, because buying committees need
   material to convince their boss.
   → Use usage numbers (100,000+ students and faculty), audit outcomes and savings as the proof. Each work
   card is a mini case study: problem, what we built, one metric. Add real, named testimonials only when they exist.
   Sources: https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/ ·
   https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-trust-from-b2c/ ·
   https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-convincing-decision-makers/

6. **Liking: show the real founder, in his own voice.** In eyetracking, users spent 10% more time on real
   team portraits than on the bios, which took 316% more space. They "ignore stock photos of generic people."
   Users trust sites that show "humans behind the product or service."
   → Real photo of Ahmet (`public/pngs/profileImages/Ahmet.jpeg`) next to a short first-person note.
   No stock imagery and no illustrated "team".
   Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/ ·
   https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/

7. **Show the process and who does the work.** NN/g's credibility factors include comprehensive content that
   shows "the full service process, including the people who will perform the work," plus upfront disclosure:
   contact details, costs and timeframes before the user has to commit.
   → A 4-step process with what the client gets at each step. Contact details are visible without a form.
   If Ahmet is willing, add 2–3 typical engagement shapes with price ranges ("Show prices for common
   scenarios"). **[confirm]**
   Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/ ·
   https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-prices-for-common-scenarios/

8. **Reverse risk with process, not guarantees.** B2B buyers fear switching costs and hidden scope.
   NN/g recommends addressing those worries directly. When asking for information, give the reason (Langer 1978,
   cited by NN/g).
   → Concrete, low-risk steps: a written plan before any build commitment, weekly working increments,
   code and infrastructure in the client's own accounts. These are offers Ahmet must agree to. **[confirm]**
   Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-trust-from-b2c/

9. **Use scarcity only when it's true.** Scarcity is real persuasion (Concorde sales rose when the route
   ended), but fake urgency is a named dark pattern (FTC). In Canada, a claim is judged on its **general
   impression**, not just its literal wording.
   → No countdowns and no "only 2 seats left" unless it's literally true that week. Honest scarcity that is
   true today: *you work directly with the founder, and his time is finite.* Availability wording is **[confirm]**.
   Sources: https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/ ·
   https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers ·
   https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations-and-deceptive-marketing-practices

10. **Separate employer work from studio clients.** Under the same general-impression test, a logo wall of
    PwC, Synoptek and Deop would imply they hired Myhtra. They didn't.
    → Label each card's context explicitly: *"As senior engineer at PwC Canada"*, *"Studio product"*,
    *"Led the team"*. Use no logo wall and no "Trusted by". Employer history goes in a compact "Track record"
    strip, not in "Clients".
    Source: https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/false-or-misleading-representations-and-deceptive-marketing-practices

11. **Voice: "I" for promises, "Myhtra" for the studio, "we" only when it's true.** Solo operators who say
    "our team will get back to you" create confusion about who is accountable. Switching to "I" improved
    trust because clients felt they had direct access to the expert. Established solo consultants use "I"
    in the hero and still sell to global brands (e.g. Harry Roberts: "Your website is slow. I can tell you
    why."). This is practitioner opinion, not lab research.
    → Make the studio name the subject of capability lines ("Myhtra designs, builds and runs…"). Promises are
    first person ("You work directly with me"). Flip to "we" in `_content/site.ts` the day the first member
    joins. This keeps the brand from claiming a team it doesn't have yet.
    Sources: https://www.selfemployed.com/mistakes-freelancers-make-when-trying-to-look-bigger-than-they-are/ ·
    https://csswizardry.com/

12. **Look simple, familiar and fast, because that reads as premium.** Users form an aesthetic judgment in
    17–50 ms. Low visual complexity plus a familiar (prototypical) layout scores highest. Speed moves
    conversion: Rakuten 24 saw +33% conversion after Core Web Vitals work, and the BBC lost 10% of users per extra second.
    → A conventional section order, plenty of white space, one accent colour, and no gimmicks that cost LCP.
    A fast site is also proof of the craft we sell.
    Sources: https://research.google/pubs/pub38315/ · https://web.dev/learn/performance/why-speed-matters

**Job-seeker signals we drop, and what replaces them**

| Drop (reads as résumé) | Show instead |
|---|---|
| Skill bars / % ratings (self-scored, unverifiable; ruled out in ROADMAP) | Stack tags on real work cards |
| "Open to work", "Available for hire", "Looking for opportunities" | "Start a project" + honest capacity line **[confirm]** |
| Chronological job list as the main section | Outcome cards; employers in a one-line "Track record" strip |
| Responsibilities ("Responsible for CI/CD…") | Results ("20 apps moved to .NET 6/8, deployed via CI/CD to AKS") |
| Downloadable CV as a CTA | Case-study pages (`/work/<slug>`, Phase 2) |
| "Passionate developer" self-descriptions | The client's problem, then what we did about it |

## 4. Words to avoid and tone rules

**Avoid**
- Hype: *revolutionize, cutting-edge, world-class, best-in-class, seamless, next-gen, synergy, leverage.*
- Résumé words: *responsible for, seeking, open to work, available for hire, passionate, team player, years of experience in X, Y, Z.*
- Empty superlatives: *perfect, flawless, guaranteed.* The motto carries "perfection" once, in the footer.
- Fake scale: *our team, our engineers, our clients* (until true), *trusted by* next to employer logos.
- Vague CTAs: *Get started, Learn more, Submit.*
- AI-washing: *AI-powered everything.* Say what the agent does and who approves it.

**Tone rules**
- Lead with the client's problem, then what we did, then the number.
- Use nouns and numbers over adjectives. If a sentence still works with the adjective deleted, delete it.
- Keep sentences short, in plain English. One idea per line on cards.
- "I" for promises, "Myhtra" for capability, "we" only once someone joins.
- Calm confidence: no exclamation marks, no emoji, no "rockstar/ninja".
- Every claim must be one Ahmet could defend in a reference call.

## 5. Ahmet must confirm before launch

1. **Naming PwC Canada** at all, and the exact phrasing "as senior engineer at PwC Canada". Check the
   employment agreement and any outside-business or public-communication policy. Also check that running a
   studio while employed is permitted.
2. **The ~$2–3M figure.** Is it public or confidential? Keep the word "contributed". If in doubt, use the fallback proof item.
3. **Naming Synoptek and Deop**, and whether the 100,000+ college app and the 1,000+ pod scanner can be
   attributed to a specific employer (the copy above deliberately doesn't).
4. **Healtify context.** Was it client work, a venture or employment? Can it be named? Say "built to HIPAA
   requirements" unless a formal compliance assessment exists. "HIPAA-compliant" is a stronger claim.
5. **Availability wording.** "Open for new projects" or "Taking on new projects" goes live only if it's true. Decide
   whether a status chip appears at all.
6. **Reply-time promise** (current code says "within two working days").
7. **Seat count.** Current code has `openSeats: 2`. Remove it unless there are two real roles.
8. **Risk-reversal offers** in Process: written plan before build, fixed price vs rate, client-owned
   accounts and code, optional ongoing run. Keep only the ones he will honour.
9. **Price ranges** for 2–3 typical engagements, if he's willing to publish them (principle 7).
10. **Contact address.** A `hello@myhtra.co` mailbox reads more like a studio than a personal Hotmail address.
11. **Business name.** Confirm Myhtra Studios is registered (e.g. Ontario business name) before calling it a studio in metadata.
12. **Book project.** Is he comfortable showing a novel-writing project on a client-facing site? It's an
    honest "agents that ask first" story, but it's optional.
13. **Founder photo.** Is the current `Ahmet.jpeg` recent and professional enough to carry the "liking" principle?
